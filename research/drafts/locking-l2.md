# Locking by object inside a node (variant L2)

**What this is.** The variant L2 of the locking inside a node of several cores, a lock per object, as [kernel-interface.md](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md) wrote it from D-129 (16 November 2026) to D-137 (pass 19, 17 November 2026): the order by kind, the rule of tries and its `again`, what each call tries, the quiet word and its escalation, the entry word of each core, the bound *k* of each call, and the L2 terms of the bounds and of the start-up checks. **It is not the contract.** D-138 (17 November 2026) took it out of the contract of version 1; the text is kept here, unchanged, so that it can come back as an addition and not be written again.

**Why it left.** Three passes of reviews (17, 18 and 19) found their critical faults in the machinery that L2 needs and L1 does not: the rule of tries, the `again` it adds to fourteen calls, the quiet word, the store-load barriers of the entry word, the bound *k*. The contract takes L1 (one queue lock per node, held for one kernel event) by default and L2 only if a measure on the reference desktop asks for it, and that measure is not made ([pull request 8](https://github.com/LES-RATS/dodos-kernel/pull/8) of the kernel repository, the bare-metal measurement image, open). What the measure shows will decide then: L2, smaller nodes, or something else ([Open points](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#open-points)).

**What the contract keeps for it (the sockets).** So that L2 is an addition later and not a change of format, the contract keeps reserved: the lock word inside every object and the object sizes that hold it (64 bytes for an endpoint, a notify, a reply and a timer; 256 for a `sched` and a task; the 1 KB fixed part of a thread; the header page of an aspace and of an iospace); the 16-byte directory entry of a `cspace`, whose sequence word would be its segment's lock; the eight range-lock words of an aspace header; the lock word of each core's part of the node block; the quiet word and the kernel epoch of the node block (the epoch is used under L1 too, by the watermark's barrier); the entry word of each core's part; and two reserved bits of the fixed part of a thread, for the count of `again` of the rule of tries. [Object sizes](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#object-sizes) and [A node of several cores](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#a-node-of-several-cores) say so.

**Faults known against it and not corrected.** Found by the reviews of 17 November 2026 against the text below; none was corrected, since the text left the contract in the same pass:

1. **Two cores that escalate at once deadlock on the quiet word.** An escalating core waits for its ticket from inside its event with its entry word still at the old epoch, while the holder waits for that entry word to read 0 or the new epoch ([Hydrogen's twelfth contract review](../../reviews/2026-11-17-contract-review-12-gemini-hydrogen.md), finding 1; confirmed by [DeepSeek's verification](../../reviews/2026-11-17-contract-review-12-verify-hydrogen-by-deepseek.md), which gives the correction: write the entry word 0 by an ordering operation before waiting for the ticket, and read the epoch again once served).
2. **Marks ORed without a lock lose bits when a thread changes task.** A delivery that reads the thread's new task before `task_attach` has ORed the old task's marks into it stamps marks 0, and a detach that writes the own word from the old task loses the bits a sibling's delivery ORs into the old task meanwhile (finding 2 of the same review, confirmed; the verification proposes that an attach that changes the task take the quiet word). Under L1 every delivery and every attach runs under the node lock and the fault does not exist.
3. **The bound of a request misses the event of the asked core's interrupt.** Under L2 a request lasts up to 3 × *E* + (*N* − 1) × *B*1max + *D*, not 2 × *E* + (*N* − 1) × *B*1max + *D*: after its current event and its wait for the quiet word the asked core still runs the event of the invalidation interrupt, at most *E*; the start-up check of `CORE_ANSWER_NS` and the row of [Numbers](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#numbers) carry the same error (finding 7, confirmed). The L1 half of the finding is corrected in the contract by D-138.
4. **The bound *k* undercounts.** By the verification's recount: `node_send` takes 11 (the doorbell's notify, whose list the new grant entry joins), `reply_recv` 68 (`recv` holds no gate to subtract), a step of a removal 388 (the sequence word of a dying space's first directory entry, which guards its sweep) and 420 if a unit that removes the root of a `cspace` keeps the lock of that space's dying mark (the verification's "missed" item 3); `thread_configure` must try the first directory entry of the `cspace` it leaves and the segment of the old message page's slot, which it finds through the thread it holds. `recv` (40) and a step of `task_revoke` (194) stand (finding 8, confirmed in part).
5. **One citation names an older edition.** The quiet word's paragraph quotes the Intel SDM from edition 253668-032, §8.2.3.4, against the one edition the contract names (finding 11 (a)); the section is "Loads May Be Reordered with Earlier Stores to Different Locations", to be found again in order 325462-093 before the text comes back.

The model of [pull request 3](https://github.com/LES-RATS/dodos-kernel/pull/3) checked the order of the locks, the tries, the two rounds and the escalation with a scripted second core (`Model::hold`) and explored the four orders of two cores' accesses (`locks::entry_orders`); D-138 removed that code with the variant, and the branch's history keeps it (commit `04503d5`).

**What must hold before it comes back.** (1) The measure on the reference desktop, with real interrupts and the trial's suites for L1 against nodes of one core, shows L1's cost too high for IPC between cores or for unmaps by every thread, and smaller nodes do not fix it ([Open points](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#open-points)). (2) The five faults above are corrected in this text. (3) A model with real interleavings of the cores' accesses, not a scripted second core: every lock, every try and every read of an entry word a step of its own, under x86-TSO and the Armv8 memory model, with the escalation of two cores at once among its cases. (4) A review of the text by a model that did not write it, and its verification by another.

### A node of several cores: the variants, the order of L2, the rule of tries, the watermark under both variants, the quiet word, the bound *k* and the bounds (lines 649-692)

**Locking: two variants with one layout (open point).** The lock discipline of a node of several cores is chosen after the measures on the reference desktop ([Open points](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#open-points)). Both variants use the same object sizes and fields, fixed now, so the choice changes no layout:

| Variant | What it locks | Lock-free paths | Layout it needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L1, one node lock** | One queue lock per node, held for one kernel event, from entry to exit; the gate lock is taken inside it | **Reading a capability slot**: the lookup of a call reads the sequence word of the slot's segment, the slot, then the sequence word again, and retries once if a writer was active, then takes the lock; **signalling a notify**: one atomic OR on the notify's word, the lock taken only to wake a waiter. These are the two paths the trial measured lock-free (the slot read, and the notify updated by one atomic operation, which removes the lost wake-up its test hammers): trial on the Mac M5, branch `trial-multicore`, `results-mac-m5.md`, third and fourth series | the node lock word and the gate lock word in the node block; the 16-byte directory entry of a `cspace` (a segment's address and its sequence word); the notify's word updated by atomic OR |
| **L2, a lock per object** | A lock word in each object that a call changes (endpoint, notify, reply, timer, thread, `sched`, task, aspace, iospace), the sequence word of each `cspace` segment as its writers' lock, each core's lock word for its queues and its interrupt table, and the gate lock; a call waits for its locks only in one order fixed by kind and tries a lock it finds out of that order, the gate lock last, at most *k* per call (*k* fixed by the call's rules, the gate lock counted; below, D-134) | The same two | the same, and one lock word inside each of those objects, eight range-lock words in an aspace header, the lock word of each core's part |

The lock words are reserved in every object now, inside the sizes of [Object sizes](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#object-sizes) (64 bytes for an endpoint, a notify, a reply and a timer; 256 for a `sched` and a task; the 1 KB fixed part of a thread; the header page of an aspace and of an iospace), and the `cspace` directory takes 16 bytes per 128 slots instead of 8. **Five kinds of state have no lock word of their own under L2 (D-131, D-137):** the state of an `untyped` (its watermark and clean mark, words 2 and 3 of its root capability's slot: [The capability slot](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#the-capability-slot)) is guarded by the lock of that slot's `cspace` segment; an entry of a core's interrupt table is guarded by that core's lock word, which an arrival takes on its own core and `irq_claim`, `irq_bind`, `irq_ack` and the removal steps of an `irq` or a notify take from any core; the words the gate writes are guarded by the gate lock, and so are the node's rings to other nodes, its credits and its unit request and service tables, which cores of the node write in turn (D-137: no lock was named for them); and the fields of a `cspace` that are not slots (its dying mark, its sweep, the head of its list of bound threads) are guarded by the sequence word of the first entry of its directory, which exists from the space's creation (D-137). **The gate lock is the last lock of every order:** a call takes it after the object locks of its order, takes no other lock while it holds it, and counts it among its *k*, with *h* at least the longest gate call (the eighth review's verification: an untyped has no object to hold a lock, and an arrival racing an `irq_ack` on another core lost an interrupt for ever).

**The order of L2 and its bound (gap 5, corrected by D-134).** The order by kind is: the sequence words of `cspace` segments, by space then segment (the state of an `untyped` with them, under the lock of its root's segment); task; thread; endpoint; reply; notify; timer; `sched`; aspace; iospace; the lock words of cores, by position; the gate lock. Two locks of one kind are taken in the order of their objects' addresses. **No order by kind holds for every call** (the ninth review: a delivery goes from an endpoint to the thread at the head of its queue, a destruction of an aspace from the aspace to the slots of its records, a `cap_derive` from a slot to its neighbour in the derivation list, which may lie in any segment), so the rule is:

- **A core waits for a lock only in the order by kind.** A lock that a call finds by reading an object it already holds locked, and that comes earlier in the order than a lock it holds, it **tries**: a try never waits. If the try fails, the call releases every lock it holds, having changed nothing, takes the locks it now knows it needs in the order (waiting, as for any lock taken in order), and checks that the object it read still leads to the same lock (the same thread at the head of the queue, the same neighbour in the list, the same binding). If it does not, or if a later try of that second round fails, the call returns `again` and has changed nothing. So a call makes at most two rounds, and its bound stays a bound: twice its one-round bound. **A call writes nothing before its last try has succeeded (D-135):** it reads the links it follows, takes or tries every lock its effect needs, and only then writes, so a failed try always finds it with nothing changed; the exceptions are atomic ORs that no failed try could undo and that no `again` follows: the OR of a `signal` (below) and the ORs of marks at a delivery, which come after the delivery's last try ([Tasks and marks](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#tasks-and-marks), D-137). A step of a removal keeps the rule unit by unit: a try that fails in a unit after the first ends the step after the units already done, which is the `again` of every removal step (a step was done); a try that fails in its first unit takes the second round as any call does. **What "changed nothing" means for a removal step (D-137):** no unit done. Before its first unit's try a step may write only the progress that every step of that removal writes and that a repeated call finds again: the *dying* mark of the capability under `cap_delete` and of an object whose root the removal reached, and the sweep position of a dying space; a step whose first unit's try fails in both rounds returns the `again` of tries with those and nothing else written, and the next call goes on from them.
- **The fields of a thread that waits on an object, which a wake changes** (its state of waiting, its links in the object's queue, the result and the message words it receives), **are guarded by the lock of the object it waits on** (an endpoint, a reply object, a notify), not by the thread's own lock, while it waits. So a delivery, an answer, a signal and the destruction of the object wake a thread without taking its lock; a call that acts on a waiting thread by its own capability (`thread_cancel`, `thread_suspend`, its destruction, `task_revoke`) holds the thread, takes the object it waits on (later in the order) and checks there that the thread still waits on it; if not, the wait is already over. **The reply object attached to a thread that waits to receive** is guarded in the same way, while it is attached, by the lock of the endpoint that thread waits on (D-135): every wake that makes it idle (a delivery, a `signal` on a bound notify, a `notify_bind` that delivers) holds that endpoint, so none takes the reply object's own lock, which comes after the endpoint; a removal that reaches an attached reply object holds it and tries the endpoint (earlier in the order).
- **An event that is not a call** (an interrupt arrival, a due event of a timer queue, a step of the recovery reserve) cannot return `again`. An arrival or a firing timer sets the bits in the notify's word by the atomic OR of both variants, under its core's lock (the notify cannot be destroyed meanwhile: its destruction unbinds its irqs and disarms its timers under that same core lock first), then tries the notify's lock to wake a waiter; if the try fails, it releases its core's lock, takes the notify's lock and wakes the first waiter if the word is not 0 and the notify is not dying, which needs no other lock; a bound endpoint found through the notify is tried in the same way, and if that try fails the event releases the notify, takes the endpoint then the notify, and wakes the endpoint's first receiver if the binding still holds, or the notify's own waiter otherwise. A due timer found through its core's queue (the timer comes before the core in the order) is tried; if the try fails, the event releases the core's lock, takes the timer's lock then the core's, and fires the timer only if it is still armed and due (otherwise a `timer_set` or a `timer_cancel` came first, and nothing is owed). A step of the recovery reserve whose try fails ends the step, as `again` ends a removal step.
- **`signal` never returns `again`, and `notify_bind` writes only after its last check (D-135).** A `signal` sets its bits by the atomic OR, which no failed try could undo, so it follows the path of an arrival: it takes the notify's lock (waiting: it holds no lock, since its slot was read without one), wakes the notify's first waiter if one waits, and otherwise tries the bound endpoint found through the notify; if that try fails, it releases the notify, takes the endpoint then the notify, and, if the word is not 0, wakes the endpoint's first receiver if the binding still holds, or the notify's own waiter otherwise. So it makes at most two rounds, its bound is twice its one-round bound, and it returns no `again`. Why: the tenth review found a `signal` that returned `again` with its bits already set, which "having changed nothing" could not describe, and a waiter could consume them meanwhile ([DeepSeek's tenth review](../../reviews/2026-11-17-contract-review-10-deepseek.md), finding 1; the exception its verification proposed would have kept an `again` after a change, which no call now returns). `notify_bind` with an endpoint names both objects in its arguments: it takes the endpoint then the notify, in the order, with no try, makes its checks, and only then writes the binding and, if the word is not 0 and a receiver waits, delivers the notification to the first one (its attached reply object needs no lock of its own: above). `notify_bind` with endpoint 0 finds the bound endpoint through the notify and tries it before it writes anything, so it may return `again` with nothing changed.
- **What each call tries** (every other call takes its locks in order): `call`, `recv`, `reply_recv` and `send`, the segments of the receive range of the thread found (the receiver of a delivery, the caller an answer goes to) and, for `recv` and `reply_recv`, the segments of the slots a caller found in the queue lists; `call`, `recv` and `reply_recv` that lend a context, the reply object on top of that context's chain of lends; `recv` and `reply_recv`, for the lends they release, the reply objects next to theirs in the chains of lends and the thread a released lend leaves without its context (D-137); `signal` (which never returns `again`, above), `notify_bind` with endpoint 0 and an arrival or a timer that signals a bound notify, the bound endpoint; `cap_derive`, `cap_move`, `cap_delete`, `cap_revoke`, `untyped_retype`, `irq_claim`, `memory_take`, `memory_give`, `memory_split`, `ioports_issue` and every removal step, the segment of a neighbour in the derivation list; `aspace_map`, `iospace_map`, `aspace_unmap`, `iospace_unmap`, `thread_configure` and the removal of a frame capability with a record, the segment or the thread of a neighbour in a space's list of records or of bound threads; `task_attach`, the threads next to the thread in the task's list; the removal steps of an aspace or an iospace (the slots of its records and the threads bound to it), of a `sched` (the reply objects and the receivers of its chain of lends), of a notify (its bound endpoint), of a reply object attached to a waiting thread (the endpoint it waits on) and of a task (its threads, when they come out of the order of addresses); `sched_configure` that moves a context, the holder thread found through it; `task_revoke`, a thread of the task whose address is lower than that of a thread it holds. The marks a delivery stamps and spreads need no lock and no try: they are ORed by atomic operations after the last try ([Tasks and marks](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#tasks-and-marks), D-137). **The delivery of a fault is not in this list (D-137):** it is an event, not a call, made at the faulting thread's entry with no lock held; it follows the path of an arrival: it takes the endpoint and the reply objects in the order, reads the top of the faulting thread's chain of lends and tries it, and if a try fails it releases everything, takes the locks it now knows in the order and checks the links; if they changed again, the event ends with the fault not delivered and nothing changed, and the thread executes its instruction again, which faults again. It never returns `again` (the fault's receive range is empty, so it tries no segment). **Each of these calls but `signal` may therefore return `again` with nothing changed under L2** ([Order of checks](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#order-of-checks)): for `cap_delete`, `cap_revoke`, `task_revoke`, `aspace_map`, `aspace_unmap`, `iospace_unmap`, `memory_give`, `recv` and `reply_recv` it is already among their results; for `call`, `send`, `notify_bind`, `cap_derive`, `cap_move`, `untyped_retype`, `irq_claim`, `memory_take`, `memory_split`, `ioports_issue`, `iospace_map`, `thread_configure`, `task_attach` and `sched_configure` it is new, and only under L2 (D-134; D-135: `signal` left the list, `memory_give` and `memory_split` joined it, both of which remove a capability from the derivation list). Rule 4 and the table of [Errors](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#errors) already tell every caller to repeat a call that returned `again`. **Every call of this list keeps the rule above (the assistant's check, D-135):** each follows a link it reads (the head of a queue, a neighbour in a list, a binding, a holder) and can try what it finds before its first write; `reply_recv` takes and tries the locks of its answer and of its receive before it writes either, since the answer could not be undone by an `again` of the receive; `recv` and `reply_recv` make every try before the release of the lends, which is their first write, the order of their effects being unchanged (D-137: the release came first and a later try could fail after it); `aspace_map` with a mapping made in steps tries the neighbour in its space's list at its first call, before its first leaf; `memory_give` tries before it starts the request of its core; a removal step and a step of `task_revoke` keep it unit by unit (`task_revoke`'s first unit, the flag and the marks of the threads that run, takes or tries all its locks before it writes the flag: [Tasks and marks](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#tasks-and-marks), D-137). No call of the list needs a write before a try.
- **The memory of a destroyed object is not reused while a core may still take its lock.** When the last object of an untyped is destroyed, its watermark becomes *ready to return*: the destroying core raises the node's **kernel epoch** (one word of the node block) by one, by an atomic add, which orders it before the core's later accesses, and writes the new value in word 6 of the untyped's root slot ([The capability slot](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#the-capability-slot)). The watermark returns to its start, and the clean mark with it, at the first `untyped_retype` on the untyped that finds the entry word of every other core of the node 0 or at least that value (the core is out of a kernel event, or in one it entered after the raise: below); until then `untyped_retype` on it returns `again` with nothing changed. The retyping core reads word 6 and then the entry words, each read ordered after the one before (an acquire read on ARM64 and RISC-V; plain on x86-64). So a lock taken after a release, through an object read before it, is never a word of memory reused for another object; the check of the second round then finds the object dying or the link changed. **The rule holds under both variants (D-137):** under L1 the events of a node run one at a time under the node lock, but the two lock-free paths (the slot read, which finds an object before the lock, and the atomic OR of a `signal` on a notify's word, made before it) read and write object memory outside the lock, so the watermark rule reads the entry words there too; on a node of one core it returns at once. Paid at the destruction of the last object of an untyped and at the first `untyped_retype` after it (*N* − 1 reads), never on a common path. (D-135: the earlier text read the heartbeat, which also moves when a core leaves an event and enters another, and two sentences of [Capabilities and objects](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#capabilities-and-objects) still returned the watermark at the destruction itself: [DeepSeek's tenth review](../../reviews/2026-11-17-contract-review-10-deepseek.md), finding 3.)
- **The quiet word: a call returns the `again` of the rule of tries at most twice in a row (D-135).** Each thread counts its consecutive results `again` that came from this rule (a try of the second round that failed, or a link that changed, with nothing changed); any other result of a call of that thread sets the count to 0 (two bits in the fixed part of the thread, among its reserved words). A call that would return such an `again` while the count is already 2 does not return it: it **escalates**. It releases every lock it holds, takes the node's **quiet word** (one word of the node block: a ticket lock, its next ticket and its serving ticket, a ticket taken by an atomic add so that the cores that wait for it are served in the order they came; held while the two differ), raises the node's kernel epoch by one by an atomic add, orders both before its next load (an atomic read-modify-write is such an operation on x86-64; `DMB ISH` after it on ARM64; `fence rw,rw` on RISC-V), waits until the entry word of every other core of the node reads 0 or at least the new epoch, runs the call alone, taking its locks in the order (no try can fail and no link can change, since no other core is in a kernel event), and releases the word by advancing its serving ticket, a release store (D-137: the earlier text said it cleared the word, which a ticket lock with a waiter behind the holder cannot be). **Every core reads the quiet word when it enters the kernel** for a call or an event, after it has written its entry word by an operation that orders that store before its next load: on x86-64 a locked exchange (`XCHG`), whose order no plain store gives, since "the Intel-64 memory-ordering model allows a load to be reordered with an earlier store to a different location" (Intel SDM, section "Loads May Be Reordered with Earlier Stores to Different Locations", read in the older edition 253668-032, §8.2.3.4, not in the edition named above); on ARM64 the store then `DMB ISH`; on RISC-V the store then `fence rw,rw` (D-137). If another core holds the word, the core writes its entry word 0, waits until the word is released and enters again, and it takes no lock before. **A core that waits for the quiet word keeps its interrupts masked (D-137):** no arrival is taken while it waits, every interrupt stays pending at its controller and is taken after the core has entered again, and an arrival that made the core enter is carried out from its step 1 once it has entered again ([Interrupts](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#interrupts)); so no step of an arrival runs while the word is held by another core, and nothing an arrival changes (the irq's state, a level line's mask, a request to the IOAPIC's holder) is left half done (the earlier text acknowledged and masked at the controller and kept the rest for later, which had no storage for the vectors kept and let an `irq_ack` or an `irq_bind` of another core run between the halves: [Hydrogen's verification of the eleventh review](../../reviews/2026-11-17-contract-review-11-verify-mistral-by-hydrogen.md), B.1.2). The stop of a core (the fence's signal: an NMI, SDEI event 0 or the RISC-V stop; and in a development image the stub's) is not masked by this, takes no lock, and is handled by its handler as anywhere else. What waits meanwhile, each at most the wait for the quiet word below: the core's heartbeat, which it writes at the exit of its next event (`STALE_NS` is checked above it: [Numbers](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#numbers)); an invalidation interrupt, so a request outstanding when the word is taken completes after it is released (its bound below has the term); the due events of its timer queue, the sweep and the start's retries among them, whose lateness is an interrupt's latency. **The entry word** of each core, in its part of the node block, holds the kernel epoch the core read when it entered its current kernel event, and 0 outside one and while it waits for the quiet word: one ordered write to the core's own line at each entry, one release store of 0 at each exit (on ARM64 `STLR`, on RISC-V `fence rw,w` then the store, plain on x86-64, so every access of the event is done before a reader sees 0; it needs no store-load order, since a stale non-zero only delays a reader), and one read of the epoch, a word written only at an escalation and at the last destruction of an untyped, so nothing on a memory access. It is what both rules read to know that every other core has ended the kernel event it was in (an escalation, and the return of a watermark above); the heartbeat, written at the exit of every event, cannot tell a core that runs a thread from one inside a long event. **The four orders (D-137).** Let core *a* enter (it writes its entry word, then reads the quiet word) while core *b* escalates (it takes the word and raises the epoch, then reads *a*'s entry word); with both ordering operations the four accesses happen in a single order that respects each core's program order, so one of these holds: (1) *a* reads the word before *b* takes it: *a* enters, and *b* reads *a*'s entry word non-zero and below the new epoch, and waits for *a*'s exit; (2) *b* takes the word between *a*'s write and *a*'s read: *a* reads it held, writes 0 and waits, and *b* reads either *a*'s epoch, and waits until that 0, or the 0; (3) *b* takes the word and reads *a*'s entry word between *a*'s two accesses: *b* reads *a*'s epoch and waits; *a* reads the word held and writes 0; (4) *b* takes the word and reads before *a* writes: *b* reads 0 and runs alone, and *a*, whose read comes after *b*'s take, reads the word held and waits. In no order does *a* take a lock while *b* runs alone; without the two operations, *a* could read the word free while *b* read *a*'s entry word as 0, the store-buffer outcome that each of the three architectures allows ([Hydrogen's verification of the eleventh review](../../reviews/2026-11-17-contract-review-11-verify-mistral-by-hydrogen.md), B.1.1). The watermark rule has the same shape: the destroying core's atomic add and the retyping core's ordered reads against the entering core's ordered write. **Bound of an escalated call (D-137, in place of a bound that named itself):** its one-core bound, plus *E*, plus (*N* − 1) × *B*1max, where *B*1max is the longest one-core bound of a call of the image and *E* the longest kernel event of another core of the node, its waits for locks included (its one-core bound plus 2 × *k*max × (*N* − 1) × *h*): the holder waits once for the events the other cores are in (they end in parallel, and while the word stays held from one ticket to the next no core enters an event, so a run of escalations pays *E* once), and each escalation ahead of it in the queue, at most *N* − 1, runs alone in its one-core bound. **What it costs the other cores:** a kernel entry may wait for the quiet word for at most *E* + (*N* − 1) × *B*1max, a term of every bound below; a wait for the quiet word longer than `CORE_ANSWER_NS` is a failure of the node, as a lock wait is. **Who pays the waits (D-137):** a wait for a lock or for the quiet word is kernel time, charged to the context that was running when the core entered the kernel, as every kernel time of an event is ([What runs next](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#what-runs-next)); admission leaves no time free (a core's sum may reach a whole core), so the wait is taken from that context's budget, bounded per entry by the bounds below, as the time of an interrupt is (the earlier text said it was a term of "the time admission leaves free", which does not exist). **How often:** an escalation costs the escalating thread three calls of its own budget (the two `again` and the escalated call), so a context of budget *b* per period makes at most *b* / (3 × *c*) escalations per period, *c* the shortest kernel time of a call. **What the ordering costs (D-137):** one ordering operation per kernel entry of a core of a node of several cores, under both variants (under L1 the entry word is written for the watermark rule, whose readers the lock-free paths make necessary, and there is no quiet word to read), and none on a node of one core, which has no entry word; the trial that chooses between L1 and L2 measures it ([Open points](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#open-points)). Why: a core that keeps changing the link a call reads could otherwise make it return `again` without end ([DeepSeek's tenth review](../../reviews/2026-11-17-contract-review-10-deepseek.md), finding 5, and its verification); the verification's rule, taking the lock that failed first in a third round, is what the second round already does and bounds nothing. Under L1 no try exists, so no call escalates.

**Why no call deadlocks under it.** A deadlock needs a cycle of cores, each waiting for a lock that the next one holds. A core waits only for a lock that comes after every lock it holds in the order: a try never waits, and a failed try releases everything before anything is waited for. Take a core of such a cycle: it holds *a* and waits for *b*, with *a* < *b*; the core holding *b* waits for some *c* > *b*, and so on round the cycle, which would have to increase strictly and come back to *a*: impossible in a total order. The queue locks of the bounds below serve each waiter after at most *N* − 1 holders, and the gate lock stays last, with no lock taken after it. **The quiet word adds no cycle (D-135):** a core waits for it only at the entry of an event, before it takes any lock, its entry word 0; the holder takes it only once it has released every lock, waits only for the other cores' current events, none of which waits for it, and then takes its locks in the order with no other core in an event; and it holds the word across no wait for another core or for a unit (a call that must wait for its node's cores or for a unit enters that wait at the end of its event and releases the word first, so the cores it asked can answer). **What the two rules prove together:** every call ends, and a call repeated after the `again` of tries succeeds at its third attempt at the latest; the reference model checks the order of the locks each event takes, tries apart from waits, with tries that fail, the second round and the escalation.

**The bound *k* (counted again by D-137).** A call's *k* is the most locks it holds at once in its second round, the locks it found included, counted from the list of tries above and from the locks each effect needs; it counts one lock per core whose queues or interrupt table the call may change and one for the gate lock (`locks::bound` of the reference model). The marks of a delivery take no lock (above). The pieces, each from a rule of this contract: **placing** a capability after its parent takes 3 segments (the destination, the parent's, and that of the parent's old next in the derivation list); **moving** one takes 4 segments (the source, the destination, its previous and its next in the derivation list), and for a frame capability whose record is full 2 more (its neighbours in its space's list of records) and the space whose head may change, or the thread whose message page binding names the slot: 7; **removing** one, as a unit of a removal, takes 3 segments (its slot, its previous and its next) and, for a full record, 2 more segments, the space, the gate and the lock of the caller's core for its invalidation request: 8; a **wake** across cores takes the lock of the woken context's core and the gate (for the reschedule interrupt), and making the calling thread wait takes its own core's lock. Four capabilities of a message moved, the worst case: 4 sources, the receive range's 4 consecutive slots in at most 2 segments, 8 neighbours in the derivation list, 8 neighbours in lists of records, 4 spaces or threads: **26**. **Bounds (D-042) as a function of *N*.** Every lock of a node is a queue lock: a waiter waits only for the holders ahead of it, at most *N* − 1, each holding it once. Let *H* be the longest hold of the node lock (L1) and *h* the longest hold of an object lock (L2), each on the slowest kind of core in the node: a lock held by an efficiency core delays a performance core waiting for it ([heterogeneous-cores.md](heterogeneous-cores.md)). Then:

- with L1, a call's bound is its one-core bound plus (*N* − 1) × *H*;
- with L2, a call that takes *k* locks is bounded by its one-core bound plus 2 × *k* × (*N* − 1) × *h*: at most two rounds of locks, by the rule of tries above (D-134);
- with L2, every call and every event may first wait for the quiet word, at most *E* + (*N* − 1) × *B*1max (above, D-135, D-137); with L1 there is no quiet word;
- a lock-free read retries once and then takes the lock, so it is bounded as a locked call;
- **no lock is held across a wait for another core or for a unit (D-131).** A wait for the completion of a core's invalidation request is a state of the waiting thread, *waiting for its node's cores*, entered at the end of one kernel event and left at another; a wait for an IOMMU unit is *waiting for a unit*, and the holder of the unit checks for the confirmation in later events, never inside one ([IOMMU](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#iommu)); so *H* and *h* hold no wait for another core and no wait for hardware. Each asked core answers at the interrupt sent to it, which it takes at the latest at the end of its current kernel event (an idle core is not asked: [TLB](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#tlb-one-core-and-several)); so a wait for a request lasts at most *N* × *H* (L1), or *E* for the asked core's current event plus *E* + (*N* − 1) × *B*1max for a wait for the quiet word at its next entry (L2: 2 × *E* + (*N* − 1) × *B*1max, D-137: the earlier bound left the quiet word out), plus *D*, the delivery of one interrupt, every core answering in parallel. The calls that wait so are `aspace_unmap`, `aspace_protect` and `memory_give` on x86-64 and RISC-V; a removal step never waits for another core (it returns `again`), and a call that must wait for a thread to leave another core returns `again` (below). A call that both takes locks and waits is bounded by the sum of the two bounds;
- **a lock wait, a wait for the quiet word, or a request not complete, longer than `CORE_ANSWER_NS`** (10 ms, provisional: above the longest firmware stall of a core, as for `STALE_NS`, so that a core held in system management mode is not taken for a dead one) is a failure of the node (below). So every wait of a kernel event is bounded by construction, whatever the cores do; a unit that does not confirm is not a failure of the node ([IOMMU](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#iommu)). **The machine start-up refuses a `CORE_ANSWER_NS` not above the longest healthy wait it judges, for the largest *N* the kernel image supports (entry `Node`), from the values measured for that image (D-131, D-137):** under L1, *N* × *H* + *D* (a request is the longest of the three waits: a lock wait is at most (*N* − 1) × *H*); under L2, 2 × *E* + (*N* − 1) × *B*1max + *D*, with *E* = *E*1 + 2 × *k*max × (*N* − 1) × *h*, *E*1 the longest kernel event on one core and *k*max the largest *k* of the image's calls (above), which bounds a lock wait of two rounds, a wait for the quiet word and a request with one interrupt delivery. *H*, *h*, *E*1, *B*1max and *D* are measured for the image, as *H* was ([Open points](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#open-points)). Otherwise ordinary contention would make a healthy node accuse itself (the eighth review found the threshold crossed at eleven cores with a hold of 1 ms, and at three with the threshold at the low end of its range; the eleventh review's verification found the earlier check, (*N* − 1) × *H*, below the request's own bound and blind to every term of L2: [Hydrogen](../../reviews/2026-11-17-contract-review-11-verify-mistral-by-hydrogen.md), Part A, finding 2).

### Marks ORed without a lock (line 1116)

**Marks only gain bits, so they are ORed without a lock (D-137).** Every writer of a marks word only adds bits: `task_mark` and the sticky rule OR into a task's word; `task_attach` ORs the thread's effective marks into the new task's word; detaching writes the thread's own word to the marks of the task it leaves, which already hold every bit of that own word (it was ORed into the task at the attach, and grows only by ORs), so it is an OR too; a task and a thread start at 0; the destruction of a task detaches its threads by the same OR; no rule clears a bit. So every write of a marks word is an atomic OR, every read is a plain read, and a delivery needs no lock of a task or a thread and no try: it reads the marks and ORs them after its last try, where nothing can follow with `again` ([A node of several cores](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#a-node-of-several-cores)). **Where a delivery ORs (D-137):** into the thread's own word always, and into its task's word too if the thread is attached to one, the task read after the own word is written; and `task_attach` writes the thread's new task first, then ORs into the new task the thread's own word and the marks of the task it leaves, each of these an operation ordered with the next (an atomic OR, a read after a store ordered as the entry word is). So when a delivery races an attach on another core, either the attach reads the own word with the new bits, or the delivery reads the new task and ORs into it: no bit a delivery stamps is lost by the thread or by the task it ends in. The own word of an attached thread is read by no rule, so ORing into it changes no result. Cost: one or two atomic ORs at a delivery whose stamp has a bit that the receiver (or, for capabilities, the sender) lacks; none at a delivery of marks 0. Why: the eleventh review's verification counted the task and thread locks that a delivery would need under L2 ([Hydrogen](../../reviews/2026-11-17-contract-review-11-verify-mistral-by-hydrogen.md), B.2.1); since no bit is ever cleared, none is needed.

### The first call of `task_revoke` under L2 (line 1144)

> **The first call's work is bounded by the cores, not by the threads (D-137):** it reads which thread each other core of the node runs (at most *N* − 1, without a lock), takes the task's lock, then the locks of those of them that are of the task, in the order (threads by address), then the locks of their cores, then the gate; it checks under the cores' locks that each still runs the thread it read, and tries the thread a core now runs if it is of the task (found through the core, so out of the order); if a try fails in both rounds, the call returns the `again` of tries with nothing written. Only then does it write the flag and the marks and send the leave interrupts, through the gate. That is the first call's only unit: it detaches nothing, so no try follows its first write; the detaching begins at the second call.

### The open points (lines 2296 and 2301)

- **Locking inside a node of several cores** (D-129). Two variants are written with one layout ([A node of several cores](../../architecture/kernel-interface.md#a-node-of-several-cores)): L1, one queue lock per node held for one kernel event, with the lock-free slot read and the lock-free signal; L2, a lock per object, with the same two lock-free paths. What decides: on the reference desktop, with a kernel of 2 to 6 cores and real interrupts, the trial's suites (lookups with changes, IPC between cores, signals from many threads, unmaps by every thread, a process beside others) for L1 and L2 against the same number of one-core nodes, weighted by the rates of `mmap`, `munmap`, `mprotect`, waits and calls traced from Ladybird and an inference engine on 4 to 8 cores; the uncontended and contended hand-over of a queue lock between performance and efficiency cores; the longest hold of each lock, which sets the bounds of D-042; the ordering operation of each kernel entry (a locked exchange, `DMB ISH`, `fence rw,rw`), paid under both variants on a node of several cores, and under L2 the waits for the quiet word (D-137). L1 is the default for the first kernel of *N* > 1 (it keeps every kernel event atomic, as the reference model assumes, and the trial found its slot read did the work of the fine locks for lookups); L2 is taken where a measure shows L1's IPC or unmap cost too high.
- **The longest hold *H* of a node's lock** on each kind of core, and under L2 *h*, *E*1, *B*1max and the delivery *D* of one interrupt, which bound the cores per node an image may support (D-131, D-137: `CORE_ANSWER_NS` must stay above *N* × *H* + *D* under L1 and 2 × *E* + (*N* − 1) × *B*1max + *D* under L2); to measure with the trial of the locks below, which also measures the ordering operation of each kernel entry of a node of several cores (D-137).
